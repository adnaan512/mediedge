1. Executive Summary & Clinical Problem Formulation

Modern deep learning models achieve radiologist- and dermatologist-level diagnostic accuracy. However, state-of-the-art vision models (e.g. Vision Transformers, deep ResNets) are engineered under the assumption of high-end GPUs, copious RAM, and continuous gigabit broadband connectivity for cloud inference.

In rural and primary-care settings across Pakistan and the broader Global South, this operational reality does not exist. Primary care clinics typically operate under severe resource constraints:

  • Low-Cost Compute: Standard consumer-grade CPUs (Intel Core i3/i5, 4 GB RAM) with zero dedicated GPU accelerators.
  • Intermittent / Zero Internet: Frequent broadband blackouts preclude cloud API reliance, requiring fully offline on-device computation to guarantee patient data confidentiality.
  • Power Instability: Energy-efficient local inference is necessary to operate within limited battery or backup power budgets.
Core Research Question: Can an edge-optimized, compressed deep learning architecture match a heavy standard model's diagnostic accuracy at a fraction of the memory footprint and CPU latency?

The Answer: Yes. Evaluated on the standardized MedMNIST DermaMNIST clinical benchmark (N = 10,015), our compressed MobileNetV2 achieved 73.07% Test Accuracy and 0.9078 Macro AUC at an on-disk footprint of just 2.53 MB. This achieves a 16.9× compression ratio compared to the ResNet-18 baseline (42.70 MB), maintaining diagnostic performance within 5 percentage points (4.84 points: 73.07% vs. 77.91%) of the heavy baseline and matching uncompressed MobileNetV2 with sub-30ms CPU latency.

2. Experimental Setup & Reproducibility Specification

  • Benchmark: MedMNIST v2 — DermaMNIST (7 classes, 28×28 RGB). Fixed splits: 7,007 train, 1,003 validation, 2,005 test images.
  • Framework: PyTorch 2.1.0+cpu, Torchvision 0.16.0.
  • Epochs: 10 epochs across all models (standardized for equitable relative comparison).
  • Batch Size: 64.
  • Optimization: Adam optimizer (lr = 0.001, weight decay = 1e-4) with Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule (T_max = 10).
  • Early Stopping: Patience = 4 epochs monitoring validation accuracy.
  • Random Seed: Seed 42 fixed across all runs.
  • Hardware Profile: Intel Core x86 CPU, 4 GB RAM, Windows 11 OS (zero GPU acceleration). Latency benchmarked over 100 timed iterations.

Semester 1 (Months 1–6): Sub-8-Bit Extreme Quantization & Algorithmic Foundations
Investigate ultra-low precision quantization (INT4 / mixed-precision FP4/INT8) and Hardware-Aware Neural Architecture Search (HW-NAS) tailored to ARM Cortex-A and Intel low-power CPUs. Complete comprehensive literature survey and benchmark on 2D MedMNIST suites.
Semester 2 (Months 7–12): Volumetric 3D Medical Imaging & Edge Runtimes
Expand compression techniques to 3D clinical imaging volumes (OrganMNIST3D, Synapse CT, BraTS MRI) using 3D depthwise separable convolutions and sparse tensor runtimes (ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO, TensorRT). Target sub-100ms volumetric slice segmentation on low-cost CPUs.

Develop decentralized, communication-efficient federated learning protocols enabling edge devices in decentralized clinics to collaboratively train models without transmitting sensitive patient records. Submit paper to flagship conference (MICCAI / IEEE ISBI).

Package the complete stack into an open-source, offline diagnostic edge toolkit; conduct validation trials in partner primary-care clinics in Pakistan. Complete Master's thesis dissertation and submit comprehensive journal article to IEEE JBHI.
